Favicon · optical vs geometric centering

Centering the ink, not the box

Your instinct is right: a glyph centered by its bounding box often looks off-center, because the left and right halves don't contain the same amount of ink. Each favicon below shifts the glyph so the horizontal centroid (area-weighted center) sits at the box center. The shift is computed at load time — a hidden canvas rasterizes each glyph and averages the ink pixels.

symmetric — no shift needed

Numbers under each mark are the actual pixel shift applied to move the ink centroid to the box center, measured against the glyph size. Symmetric letters (bare m, uppercase M) come out near 0. Adding a period or accent pushes the shift by several percent — visible to the eye once you know to look.
